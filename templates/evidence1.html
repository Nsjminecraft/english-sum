Core Concept
CORE CONCEPT

The concept that had the absolute biggest impact on me this semester was learning how to structure my thoughts using the P.E.A. (Point, Evidence, Analysis) framework. Before this, my writing was basically just a massive brain dump. I had plenty of ideas, but they would tumble onto the page all at once, and I constantly found myself jumping to completely random topics mid-paragraph just because they popped into my head. This framework gave me a simple, reliable blueprint that completely fixed that habit and kept my writing locked into one clear focus.

a Course Resource
A Course Resource

The resource that completely turned things around for me was the slide deck called ENL1W0- PEA. This lesson broke down exactly how a strong paragraph needs to be split up into three clear steps: introducing a specific point, blending a quote smoothly into a sentence, and writing a few solid lines of analysis to connect them. Seeing it broken down visually like that made me realize exactly where my unstructured writing was failing so I could actually fix it.

What I Learned
What I Learned

I learned that good writing isn't about just generating sentences until a paragraph looks full; it’s about treating a paragraph like a strict boundary zone. By forcing myself to establish a single, clear point right at the start, I learned how to filter out mental clutter. If a random thought enters my head while I’m writing, I can look back at my point and immediately know it doesn't belong there. It forced me to stop taking shortcuts and actually finish one complete thought before moving on to the next.

How it Influenced My Perspectives

How it Influenced My Perspectives

This completely shifted how I look at the whole writing process. I used to think writing was just about stating an opinion and hoping the reader got it. Now, I see it as an organized system where every single sentence has a specific job to do. The analysis step, in particular, completely changed my perspective because it forces me to sit down and write a few extra sentences explaining why my evidence matters instead of just scratching the surface. It has made me a much more deliberate, detailed writer, not just in English class, but across all my subjects.
